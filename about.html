The Artist Behind the Wood

Cassy MacDonald

Somewhere in the wooded hills north of Concord, New Hampshire, there's a small workshop that smells of cedar shavings, wood stain, and something harder to name. That's where Cassy MacDonald works — and has worked, in one form or another, since she was old enough to hold a chisel.

She didn't go to art school. She went to the forest. The craft came from watching, doing, failing, and doing again — from a grandmother who carved spoons and a father who built furniture in the garage on winter weekends.

Her work is rooted in New England — the particular texture of its winters, its stone walls and pine forests, its old stubbornness — but it reaches for something stranger. Something a little wary.

From Tree to Table

The Process

No two pieces start or end the same way. But there's a rhythm to how Cassy works.

01

Finding the Wood

Most pieces start with salvaged, fallen, or locally sourced New England hardwoods — birch, maple, cherry, cedar. The tree tells her what it wants to be.

02

Sketching the Form

Cassy sketches loosely — more mood than blueprint. She lets the grain of the wood shift the design. Plans change when the material speaks.

03

Carving & Shaping

Hand tools when possible. Router, lathe, and band saw when needed. The work is slow. Cassy doesn't rush.

04

Burning & Painting

Pyrography adds depth and detail — burned lines that can't be undone. Select pieces receive hand-painted accents, particularly the Cassowary blues.

05

Finishing

Oil, wax, or matte lacquer — chosen to let the wood breathe and age. Each finish is applied by hand, multiple coats, light sanding between each.

06

Sent Into the World

Every piece ships wrapped in kraft paper, with a hand-written note from Cassy about that specific piece — its wood, its quirks, its name.

What She Works With

The Materials

🌿
White Birch

New Hampshire's signature tree. Pale, clean grain. Great for signs and relief panels.

🍁
Sugar Maple

Hard, fine-grained Vermont maple. Takes pyrography beautifully. Warm honey tone.

🌲
Eastern Cedar

Aromatic and richly colored. Perfect for totems, boxes, and objects meant to last.

🍒
Wild Cherry

Darkens with age to a deep reddish-brown. Cassy's favorite for keepsake boxes.

🪨
Driftwood

Gathered from Maine's coast. Weathered, sculptural. Each piece is already half-art.

✨
Resin & Pigment

For inlays and accents — glow pigments, midnight blues, forest greens.
